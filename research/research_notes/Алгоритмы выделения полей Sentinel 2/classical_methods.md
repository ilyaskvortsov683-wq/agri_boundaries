# Classical (non-deep-learning) and weakly-supervised methods for agricultural field boundary delineation from Sentinel-2 / Landsat time series

Access note for the report writer: in this session direct fetching of publisher pages (ScienceDirect, MDPI, arXiv, IEEE, university repositories) was blocked by the network proxy. Paper-level facts below come from search-engine abstracts and snippets, so they are abstract-level and some numbers need checking against the full text. Code-level facts (GitHub) were checked directly by cloning the repositories and reading the source. Facts checked that way are marked "(verified in code)".

## Q1. What are the main classical / weakly-supervised methods, and what is their reproducible methodology (inputs, edges, segmentation, post-processing, parameters, accuracy)?

### Takeaway
The best-documented classical family for S2/Landsat time series has three steps. (1) Compute an edge or gradient layer for each date, or on several composites. (2) Aggregate it over time with a median, mean or sum. (3) Segment that layer: watershed, a hierarchy or region merging, or active contours. Then intersect the result with a cropland mask and vectorise it. Watkins & van Niekerk's CEWS (Canny + watershed), Graesser & Ramankutty's multi-temporal edge-gradient magnitude + CLAHE + adaptive threshold, North et al.'s temporal-local-std + directional filters thresholded by length, and Yan & Roy's active-contour + watershed are the main reference points. MRS/eCognition optimised with Bayesian search (Tetteh) is the strongest OBIA baseline that has been benchmarked against DL.

### Cited Findings

**Watkins & van Niekerk 2019a (Comput. Electron. Agric. 158:294–302) and 2019b (CEA 167:105078). CEWS = Canny edges + watershed on multi-temporal S2**
- Citation for 2019a: "A comparison of object-based image analysis approaches for field boundary delineation using multi-temporal Sentinel-2 imagery", CEA 158:294–302, doi 10.1016/j.compag.2019.02.009 — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-comparison-of-object-based-image-analysis-for-Watkins-Niekerk/6cdbaa7085e748f5bf1d0fb7581618c0fcb3f1a0); [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/WatkinsN19)
- Edge detection was run on multiple Sentinel-2 images in the Vaalharts irrigation scheme in South Africa. The multi-temporal edge layers were aggregated "to enhance field edges while reducing within-field heterogeneity". Watershed, multi-resolution and multi-threshold segmentation were then compared on that aggregated edge layer — [ResearchGate: Automating field boundary delineation…](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337182705_Automating_field_boundary_delineation_with_multi-temporal_Sentinel-2_imagery)
- Watershed was the most effective of the three segmentations at producing clear boundaries, according to a later paper that cites it — [Zhu et al. 2024, GF-AFD (BNU PDF)](https://srs.bnu.edu.cn/docs/2025-04/c952c0564b7644d4be31748169488727.pdf)
- The Stellenbosch thesis by the same author crossed 2 edge-enhancement algorithms (Canny, Scharr) with 3 segmentations (watershed, multi-threshold, multi-resolution), giving 6 scenarios. Canny + watershed (CEWS) was best and was then applied to 5 South African regions. The thesis also states that "there are currently no well-established and transferable techniques to automatically delineate agricultural field boundaries" — [SUN Scholar thesis record](https://scholar.sun.ac.za/handle/10019.1/107064)
- In 2019b, CEWS was modified and applied to 5 agricultural landscapes. It beat a standard supervised per-pixel classification on average, with mean absolute error about 31 m lower and overall accuracy about 13.8% higher — [ScienceDirect abstract (CEA 167:105078)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169919310403)
- The accuracy figures conflict across sources. One reports CEWS at 92.9% for mapping field boundaries (Scharr-based edges 92.7%) — [ResearchGate pre-print](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337533060_PRE-PRINT_AUTOMATING_FIELD_BOUNDARY_DELINEATION_WITH_MULTI-TEMPORAL_SENTINEL-2_IMAGERY); [LinkedIn summary](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/field-boundary-delineation-multi-temporal-sentinel-2-imagery-keith). Another source quotes a 78% boundary accuracy — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169919310403). These probably measure different things or come from different papers.
- A known weakness of watershed approaches like this: seeding a watershed from every local minimum heavily over-segments. Merging by removing shared edges below a boundary-strength threshold is unstable, because the threshold varies between regions. GF-AFD addresses this with hierarchical merging, which gave "stable parameters across regions and models", plus an OWT step to enhance weak boundaries — [Zhu et al. 2024, Int. J. Digital Earth 17(1), doi 10.1080/17538947.2023.2297947](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2023.2297947); [BNU PDF](https://srs.bnu.edu.cn/docs/2025-04/c952c0564b7644d4be31748169488727.pdf)

**Yan & Roy 2014 (RSE 144:42–64) and 2016 (RSE 172:67–86). WELD Landsat time series, variational region-based active contour + watershed**
- An automated method extracts crop fields from 30 m WELD time series. It has 3 key components: (1) multi-temporal input, which handles crop rotations and cloud gaps; (2) a variational region-based geometric active contour that "requires no training data" and has "only a small number of parameters"; (3) a watershed algorithm that splits connected segments covering several fields into individual fields. It was tested on three 150×150 km WELD tiles (Texas, California, South Dakota) containing rectangular, centre-pivot and irregular fields. Fields that were visually apparent were extracted with limited omission and commission errors relative to the USDA CDL — [OpenPrairie record](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/gsce_pubs/45/); [LCLUC document page](https://lcluc.umd.edu/documents/yan-l-and-roy-dp-2014-automated-crop-field-extraction-multi-temporal-web-enabled-landsat)
- The 2016 refinement was applied to weekly WELD (Landsat 5 TM + 7 ETM+) over the whole CONUS for December 2009 to November 2010. It extracted 4,182,777 fields, with reported mean and median sizes of 0.193 km² and 0.278 km². The size histogram peaks at 0.25×0.25, 0.25×0.5, 0.5×0.5 and 0.5×1 mile fields. Validation used 48 sites of about 7.5×7.5 km in the top 16 cropland states, which hold 76% of US harvested cropland. The method starts from per-pixel maps of crop-agriculture probability and field-edge probability, using a weighted edge-probability formulation — [OpenPrairie](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/gsce_pubs/38/); [LCLUC metadata](https://lcluc.umd.edu/metadata/conterminous-united-states-conus-field-extraction); [LCLUC doc](https://lcluc.umd.edu/documents/yan-l-and-d-p-roy-conterminous-united-states-crop-field-size-quantification-multi-temporal)

**Graesser & Ramankutty 2017 (RSE 201:165–180, doi 10.1016/j.rse.2017.08.027). Landsat field parcels across South America**
- The method combines multispectral edge extraction, multi-scale contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation (CLAHE) and adaptive thresholding on Landsat surface-reflectance (CDR) time series. It was tested on a region holding about 82% of the 2000/2001 South American cropland area. Reported results: overall cropland F-score 91% and low geometric error over 5,480 fields — [UBC Open Collections record](https://open.library.ubc.ca/cIRcle/collections/52383/items/1.0378890); [Graesser publications page](https://jgrss.github.io/science.html)
- Russia/Kazakhstan: none of the sources found mention a test region in Russia or Kazakhstan. Every description is South America — [search synthesis of citing works, e.g. Delineate Anything](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02534v1). The code README (below) has an example for "MGRS grid 11SPS over Europe" and one for South America, and nothing for Russia or Central Asia — [jgrss/sacfei README](https://github.com/jgrss/sacfei)
- Code (verified in code): Jordan Graesser's MIT-licensed repo `jgrss/sacfei`, "Semi-Automatic Crop Field Extraction from Imagery". It is marked "no longer maintained", with the last commit on 2022-03-30. Its GEE script `sacfei/earth_engine.py` smooths each image with `ee.Kernel.gaussian(15, 0.5)` and then `ee.Kernel.gaussian(9, 0.75)`. It computes an "edge gradient magnitude" (EGM) as the mean of 5 operators: Sobel, Roberts and Prewitt magnitudes plus Kirsch and compass convolutions. EGM is computed for each Fmask-cleaned image (Landsat-8 pan band in the example), then the median over the collection is taken. The README says it also has scripts for Sentinel-2 10 m and Landsat 7/8 15 m pan. The Python side (`MTMSEGM`) pre-smooths (bilateral, window 7) and computes EGM per date, setting no-data pixels (dilated ×10) to NaN. It then takes the nan-mean over dates plus a temporal coefficient-of-variation term (std/median of the per-date EGM). Further steps: logistic scaling of EGM to an edge probability (defaults α=1.5, β=0.5), CLAHE (clip 2.0, grid 16 by default), multi-scale local adaptive thresholding (`skimage.threshold_local`), probability relaxation (relaxation labelling, `relax.py`), Canny-morphology, endpoint linking (max_gap 15), minimum line size 15 px, object cleaning, and intersection with a land-cover map (`--lc-image`). The README batch example uses `--grid-tiles 16 32 --thresh-windows 25 51 --relax-probas --apply-clean --logistic-alpha 1.5 --logistic-beta 0.7` — [github.com/jgrss/sacfei](https://github.com/jgrss/sacfei) (files `sacfei/earth_engine.py`, `sacfei/mtmsegm.py`, `sacfei/utils.py` lines ~645–681, `sacfei/relax.py`)
- Graesser's later repo `jgrss/cultionet` ("Image segmentation of cultivated land") is deep learning (PyTorch) — [github.com/jgrss/cultionet](https://github.com/jgrss/cultionet)

**North, Pairman & Belliss 2019 (IEEE JSTARS 12(1):237–251, doi 10.1109/JSTARS.2018.2884513). Multi-temporal edges, New Zealand**
- The method derives closed polygons around fields from time-series imagery. It looks for boundaries that enclose regions of low variability across the image dates, by combining local standard deviations with directional edge filters. Edge responses are thresholded mainly by length rather than strength, on the argument that long straight edges separate fields better than spectral contrast does. The raster edge map is vectorised and polygon topology is built. It was demonstrated on a 4,000 km² site with SPOT imagery and is used operationally in New Zealand "to map whole districts using imagery from several satellite sensors, with little need to vary parameters" — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329817494_Boundary_Delineation_of_Agricultural_Fields_in_Multitemporal_Satellite_Imagery); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Boundary-Delineation-of-Agricultural-Fields-in-North-Pairman/626318c67d6900a7d17cf3a1bc4acd4ac8df842e); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/fc307fab406647988e70071cb5a03eec)
- Predecessor: North, Pairman, Belliss, "Paddock segmentation using multi-temporal satellite imagery", IGARSS 2014, pp. 1596–1599. This is listed in dblp author pages returned by search; it is not clear which page is North's — [dblp 31/8996](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/pid/31/8996.html); [dblp 36/9002](https://dblp.org/pid/36/9002)

**Rydberg & Borgefors 2001 (IEEE TGRS 39(11):2514–2520) and the 2002 book chapter**
- An integrated system that combines a multispectral edge- and line-detection method built from oriented filters with a region-oriented segmentation that deliberately over-segments. Knowledge about agricultural fields is built into the merging — [Uppsala CBA annual report](https://www.cb.uu.se/annual_report/AR02html/node25.html); [reference list in Batić](https://zbornik.ftn.uns.ac.rs/index.php/zbornik/article/view/2149)

**García-Pedrero, Gonzalo-Martín & Lillo-Saavedra (IJRS 2017 38(7):1809–1819; Remote Sensing 2018 10(12):1991). Superpixels + supervised merging**
- 2017: the image is over-segmented into superpixels/watershed regions. Pairs of adjacent segments are labelled "same parcel / different parcel" and a classifier is trained on them. The classifier then performs agglomerative merging on other image regions — [ULPGC repository](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/handle/10553/55018); [Medium review of approaches](https://medium.com/geekculture/%D1%81r%D0%BE%D1%80-field-boundary-detection-approaches-and-main-challenges-46e37dd276bc)
- The features reportedly come from NDVI, NDWI and the spectral shape index (SSI), which were used to build superpixels and feature maps — [Medium (secondary)](https://medium.com/geekculture/%D1%81r%D0%BE%D1%80-field-boundary-detection-approaches-and-main-challenges-46e37dd276bc)
- 2018: "The Outlining of Agricultural Plots Based on Spatiotemporal Consensus Segmentation" extends a consensus of segmentations across time, with no training data — [doi 10.3390/rs10121991](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10121991); [UVa thesis record](https://portaldelaciencia.uva.es/documentos/65c1432a07b79a6a3decf6c9). The thesis names the main drawback of the supervised variant as its need for large amounts of annotated pairs — [UVa portal](https://portaldelaciencia.uva.es/documentos/65c1432a07b79a6a3decf6c9)

**Tetteh et al. 2020–2023. Multiresolution segmentation (MRS, eCognition) with parameter optimisation, Germany**
- Remote Sensing 2020: an unsupervised MRS parameterisation. One approach searches scale only, with shape and compactness fixed. The other uses Bayesian optimisation of all three (scale, shape, compactness) against a modified unsupervised metric. It is proposed as an alternative when no reference data exist — [MDPI 12(18):3096](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/18/3096/htm)
- CEA paper ("Optimal parameters for delineating agricultural parcels from satellite images based on supervised Bayesian optimization"): mono-temporal S2 over Lower Saxony. Optimal parameters varied considerably between grids, so no single parameter set would be optimal. Segmentation quality correlated with parcel size — [OpenAgrar PDF](https://www.openagrar.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/openagrar_derivate_00034378/dn062674.pdf); [Tetteh PhD dissertation](https://www.opendata.uni-halle.de/bitstream/1981185920/83944/1/PhD_Dissertation_ULB_Gideon_Okpoti_Tetteh_neu.pdf)
- IEEE Access 2021 9:116702–116719: S1 and S2 feature sets evaluated with supervised MRS, accumulating data from March to October 2018 — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9515974/); [Thünen PDF](https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn063902.pdf)
- PFG 2023: optimised MRS against Mask R-CNN, U-Net and FracTAL ResUNet (see Q4) — [Springer PFG](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41064-023-00247-x)

**Wagner & Oppelt 2020. Graph-based growing contours (active-contour family)**
- Remote Sensing 12(7):1205: a sub-pixel extraction method that pairs boundary detection with a modified "growing snakes" active contour (graph-based growing contours). Tested on multi-temporal S2 over 2 areas in northern Germany and designed to be independent of sensor, resolution and wavelength — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/7/1205); [Kiel repository](https://macau.uni-kiel.de/receive/macau_mods_00000815)
- 12(12):1990 adds a DL boundary detector working on RGB only. Best results: accuracy up to 88.2%, AUC up to 0.94, F1 up to 0.88 — [Kiel repository](https://macau.uni-kiel.de/receive/macau_mods_00000670)

**Persello et al. 2019 (RSE 231:111253). FCN + oriented watershed transform (OWT) + UCM-style hierarchy + combinatorial grouping (DL front-end, classical back-end)**
- FCN contour probabilities feed an oriented watershed transform that builds a segmentation hierarchy, and field segments are picked by combinatorial grouping. On WorldView-2/3 in Nigeria and Mali, F-scores were above 0.7 and 0.6. It outperformed the shallow baselines gPb-owt-ucm, SCG and MCG, which were applied to pan-sharpened bands 7, 5, 2. Evaluation used precision-recall with a tolerance for small localisation errors — [PMC6737917](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6737917/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334119980_Delineation_of_agricultural_fields_in_smallholder_farms_from_satellite_images_using_fully_convolutional_networks_and_combinatorial_grouping)
- In gPb-owt-ucm, the gPb contour detector combines local and global texture, colour and brightness cues. Spectral-clustering "globalisation" joins edge fragments into long, smooth contours, and OWT-UCM turns them into a hierarchy — [Towards Automated Cadastral Boundary Delineation from UAV Data (arXiv 1709.01813)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.01813)
- Follow-up for S2: Masoud, Persello & Tolpekin 2020 (Remote Sensing 12(1):59), SRC-Net. It predicts 5 m boundaries from 10 m S2 over Flevoland and is "only slightly inferior" to MD-FCN on 5 m RapidEye — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/1/59)

**GEE SNIC + Canny pipelines**
- Marszalek, Lösch, Körner & Schmidhalter 2020 (TUM preprint, doi 10.20944/preprints202004.0316.v1): Bavaria 2016–2018, Sentinel-2. Boundaries come from SNIC (the standard GEE clustering) plus a new Canny procedure that uses NDWI and "a compilation of several Canny edge images across the vegetation period". RGB-based Canny caught fewer details than NDWI-based Canny. SNIC alone over-segmented and did not show clear boundaries. The NDWI multi-date Canny "performed very well". No quantitative boundary accuracy was found — [Preprints.org PDF](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202004.0316/v1/download); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357910112_Multi-Temporal_Crop_Type_and_Field_Boundary_Classification_with_Google_Earth_Engine)
- Gayibov 2025 (arXiv 2502.04529, Azerbaijan, Ganja and Imishli): GEE, S2 L2A NDVI at 10 m. Settings: `ee.Algorithms.Image.Segmentation.SNIC` with size 15 px and compactness 0.5; Canny "thresholds 1.5–3.0", sigma 1.0; 3-pixel morphological closing to link broken edges; SNIC clusters vectorised — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04529v1). The evaluation is qualitative. A third-party review notes that the promised IoU/F1 validation is missing and that the parameters were tuned on the same 2 images — [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2502.04529)

**Sentinel Hub / Sinergise NIVA field delineation (DL model, but the temporal merging and vectorisation are classical and reusable)**
- The repo generates parcel contours from S2 and was used for Lithuania and Castilla y León. It is archived and runs only with the pinned package versions. Background is in the blog "Parcel Boundary Detection for CAP". Imagery comes from the Sentinel Hub Batch API, and GSAA reference parcels are needed to train a ResUNet-a — [github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation README](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation)
- Post-processing (verified in code, `fd/post_processing.py`). `TemporalMerging` keeps only dates with cloud cover below `max_cloud_coverage` (default 0.05) and full valid data. It merges per-date extent and boundary predictions into each user-defined time interval (keyed by month) using a per-pixel percentile, default 50 (the median). `CombineUpsample` computes `clip(1 + extent − boundary, 0, 2)`, upsamples bicubically ×2, smooths with a disk mean (disk 2), upsamples ×2 again and smooths with disk 4 — [fd/post_processing.py](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation/blob/main/fd/post_processing.py)
- Vectorisation (verified in code, `fd/vectorisation.py`). Tiles are blended through a GDAL VRT with linear overlap weights. `gdal_contour -i 0.6` on 500-px chunks extracts polygons above threshold 0.6, polygons crossing chunk borders are merged, and geometries are simplified with tolerance 2.5. The notebook uses buffer [200, 200] and chunk_size 500 — [fd/vectorisation.py](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation/blob/main/fd/vectorisation.py)
- A related Planet "field delineation" marker clusters agricultural pixels by spatial, spectral and temporal properties — [Planet docs](https://docs.planet.com/data/area-monitoring/markers/field-delineation/)

**Waldner & Diakogiannis 2020 (RSE, doi 10.1016/j.rse.2020.111741). DL with a classical-style temporal consensus**
- ResUNet-a predicts extent, boundary and distance-to-boundary. Instances are recovered by thresholding or watershed post-processing. "Building consensus by averaging model predictions from at least four images acquired across the season is the key to coping with the temporal variations of accuracy" — [arXiv 1910.12023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12023)

**Weak supervision: classical labels feeding DL**
- Zhang et al. 2021 (JAG vol. 105, Heilongjiang, China) used Canny + watershed (CW) to label training data automatically for a recurrent residual U-Net (see Q4 for accuracy) — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4789dbeaf5e34c5bb9c589912e3ca44f); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0303243421002646)

### Inferences
- Every successful classical method found works on multi-temporal edge evidence rather than on one composite: Watkins aggregates edge layers across dates, North uses temporal local std, Graesser takes the median/mean of per-date EGM, Marszalek compiles Canny across the season, and Yan & Roy use weekly WELD. The edge is detected in each date (or short-period composite) first, and only then aggregated.
- The usual core is "over-segment, then merge with a boundary-strength or learned criterion" (watershed or superpixels → region adjacency graph → merge), which is the same pattern as the user's SLIC → agglomerative merge. The literature's main improvements are in what the merge criterion uses (aggregated edge strength along the shared border, a learned pairwise classifier, a hierarchy cut) and in tuning parameters regionally.
- Most classical methods are regionally tuned. Tetteh found optimal MRS parameters vary between grids; GF-AFD reports watershed merge thresholds that vary by region. North et al. are the exception, reporting little parameter change across NZ districts, which they credit to thresholding by edge length.

## Q2. Which classical methods have open code usable in Colab / Google Earth Engine today?

### Takeaway
No major peer-reviewed classical S2 field-delineation paper ships a maintained, turnkey Colab/GEE pipeline. What is usable today is: GEE built-ins (`SNIC`, `CannyEdgeDetector`, convolution kernels); Graesser's unmaintained but readable `sacfei` (GEE multi-kernel temporal edge script + Python CLAHE/adaptive-threshold/relaxation segmentation); the reusable classical post-processing in Sentinel Hub's archived `field-delineation`; and general libraries (Higra for watershed hierarchies/UCM-like saliency, OpenCV's Structured Edge detector with a pretrained model, scikit-image) for building gPb/OWT-UCM-style hierarchies in Colab.

### Cited Findings
- GEE `ee.Algorithms.Image.Segmentation.SNIC` returns a `clusters` band and per-band cluster means. Parameters and defaults: `size` (seed spacing, default 5 px; no grid if `seeds` is given), `compactness` (default 1; 0 turns off spatial weighting), `connectivity` 4 or 8 (default 8), `neighborhoodSize` (tile-overlap neighbourhood, default 2×size), optional `seeds` image. The doc example uses NAIP with size 30, compactness 0.1, connectivity 8. The docs warn that parameters need image-specific tuning and that results depend on zoom/scale unless the output is reprojected, which slows computation — [GEE API docs: SNIC](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/apidocs/ee-algorithms-image-segmentation-snic)
- GEE `ee.Algorithms.CannyEdgeDetector(image, threshold, sigma=1)`: a pixel is only considered if its gradient magnitude exceeds `threshold`; `sigma` is the Gaussian pre-filter (0 = none). Output is non-zero at edges, with the gradient magnitude as value, for each input band — [GEE API docs: CannyEdgeDetector](https://developers.google.cn/earth-engine/apidocs/ee-algorithms-cannyedgedetector?hl=en)
- A community GEE example applies Canny to Landsat NDVI with `ee.Algorithms.CannyEdgeDetector(ndvi, 0.7)` and reports no accuracy — [GeoJamal GEE tutorial](https://gee.geojamal.com/2025/04/detecting-edges-with-canny-edge.html). Gayibov's NDVI pipeline uses a threshold range of 1.5–3.0 and sigma 1.0 — [arXiv 2502.04529](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04529v1). The search synthesis notes that the API takes a single threshold, so the paper's "1.5–3.0" may not map onto it directly.
- `jgrss/sacfei` (MIT, unmaintained, Python 3.6-era, Cython + numba + OpenCV + GEE Python API). The GEE script computes the per-date multi-kernel edge gradient magnitude and its temporal median; the Python CLI `batch.py` performs the segmentation block by block with land-cover intersection (verified in code) — [github.com/jgrss/sacfei](https://github.com/jgrss/sacfei)
- `sentinel-hub/field-delineation` (archived). Its classical parts can be reused without the DL model: per-period percentile merging of per-date rasters, extent/boundary combination, smoothing and upsampling, and `gdal_contour` vectorisation with seamless tile merging and simplification (verified in code) — [github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation)
- Higra (`pip install higra`, v0.6.13 on PyPI) is a C++/Python library with hierarchical watersheds (by area, volume, dynamics or a custom attribute), agglomerative clustering (single, average, complete, Ward linkage), region adjacency graphs, saliency maps (ultrametric contour maps), horizontal and optimal cuts, Mumford–Shah energy optimisation, and supervised assessment of hierarchies — [Higra README](https://github.com/higra/Higra); [PyPI higra](https://pypi.org/project/higra/)
- The official Higra "Watershed hierarchies" notebook builds the gradient with OpenCV's Structured Edge Detector (`cv2.ximgproc.createStructuredEdgeDetection(model).detectEdges(image)`). It weights a 4-adjacency graph with that gradient, builds `hg.watershed_hierarchy_by_area/volume/dynamics`, and displays `hg.saliency(tree, altitudes)`, a UCM-like boundary-strength map (verified in notebook) — [Higra-Notebooks: Watershed hierarchies.ipynb](https://github.com/higra/Higra-Notebooks)
- The pretrained Structured Edge (random-forest) model file `model.yml.gz` (about 6.4 MB) is downloadable from opencv_extra (HTTP 200 checked); it needs `opencv-contrib-python` (v5.0.0.93 on PyPI) — [opencv_extra model.yml.gz](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_extra/blob/4.x/testdata/cv/ximgproc/model.yml.gz); [PyPI opencv-contrib-python](https://pypi.org/project/opencv-contrib-python/)
- Pavlova et al. 2023 (IITP RAS, multi-temporal MSAVI2 + Otsu + Canny) state that "the dataset and a Python implementation of the algorithm are openly available" — [Computer Optics 47(3) abstract](https://computeroptics.ru/eng/KO/Annot/KO47-3/470314e.html); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371214192_Low-parameter_method_for_delineation_of_agricultural_fields_in_satellite_images_based_on_multi-temporal_MSAVI2_data)
- Small GitHub examples exist but are unreviewed. `Asimudin/field-boundary-segmentation` is a GEE workflow (Sentinel-2, NDVI, Random Forest, Flevoland). `ashikbharishivaprasad/AFB_Delineation` is a U-Net with "graph based segmentation for polygonization" — [GitHub search results: Asimudin](https://github.com/Asimudin/field-boundary-segmentation); [AFB_Delineation](https://github.com/ashikbharishivaprasad/AFB_Delineation)
- Tetteh's MRS optimisation is built on the multiresolution segmentation algorithm — [Tetteh dissertation](https://www.opendata.uni-halle.de/bitstream/1981185920/83944/1/PhD_Dissertation_ULB_Gideon_Okpoti_Tetteh_neu.pdf)

### Inferences
- In GEE, the cleanest reproducible pipeline needs no external code. Compute NDVI/NDWI/MSAVI2 for each date (or monthly medians). Run `CannyEdgeDetector` or `ee.Kernel.sobel` gradient magnitude on each, sum or median them across the season (as in Marszalek, Watkins and SACFEI), and pass the aggregated edge map plus temporal statistics into `SNIC`. Low compactness makes boundaries follow edges; `seeds` can be placed at local minima of the edge map, which makes it watershed-like. Then `reduceToVectors`. GEE has no native watershed or UCM, so hierarchical merging has to be done in Python/Colab (e.g. with Higra) after export.
- For the user's Colab pipeline, Higra is the most direct open-source way to get an OWT-UCM / gPb-UCM-style hierarchy: aggregated multi-date gradient → watershed hierarchy → saliency map → threshold or optimal cut. It can stand in for, or check, the custom SLIC + agglomerative merge.
- MRS is in practice the proprietary eCognition algorithm (background knowledge, not verified here), which hurts reproducibility in Colab. The Bayesian-optimisation idea can still be applied to any open segmenter (SLIC/Felzenszwalb/watershed parameters).

### Gaps
- No public code was found for Watkins & van Niekerk (CEWS), North et al., Yan & Roy (only the output dataset), Tetteh (eCognition rule sets) or García-Pedrero.
- The open Python implementation and the 18,859-field dataset of Pavlova et al. 2023 could not be located (GitHub repo/code search for MSAVI2/Otsu/Canny found nothing).
- No Earth Engine Community tutorial dedicated to field-boundary delineation with SNIC + Canny was found. The Sentinel Hub Medium blog could not be fetched.

## Q3. Which inputs / features are recommended (temporal std NDVI, monthly composites vs a single max/min composite, phenology metrics, harmonics)?

### Takeaway
The evidence consistently favours multi-date edge evidence and temporal-variability features over a single date. Recommended options: per-date or per-period edges aggregated by median/mean/sum; local standard deviation across dates; a temporal CV of the edge magnitude; multi-year aggregated VI history; and a water index (NDWI) or soil-adjusted index (MSAVI2) as well as NDVI. No study was found that directly compares temporal-std NDVI, max/min composites and harmonic-regression coefficients as edge inputs, so the choice between those is an evidence gap.

### Cited Findings
- North et al.: boundaries enclose "regions of low variability across the image dates", measured by combined local standard deviations plus directional edge filters — [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329817494_Boundary_Delineation_of_Agricultural_Fields_in_Multitemporal_Satellite_Imagery)
- Watkins & van Niekerk: edge layers from several S2 dates were aggregated "to enhance field edges while reducing within-field heterogeneity" — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337182705_Automating_field_boundary_delineation_with_multi-temporal_Sentinel-2_imagery)
- Marszalek et al.: an index-based (NDWI) Canny compiled across the vegetation period beat RGB Canny, and SNIC alone over-segmented — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202004.0316/v1/download)
- Graesser/SACFEI (verified in code): EGM is computed for each date and averaged (nan-mean) over valid dates. A temporal CV term (std/median of the per-date EGM) is added, and the docstring lists an "EGM CV array" and a "VI CV array" as segmentation inputs. A 3-date moving-window spectral distance (`get_mag_dist`) is also implemented — [jgrss/sacfei utils.py & main.py](https://github.com/jgrss/sacfei)
- Pavlova et al. 2021/2023 (IITP RAS): aggregated (historical, multi-year) vegetation-index data and aggregated edge maps give "much more accurate" field segmentation than an instant (single-date) VI. The 2023 version uses multi-temporal MSAVI2 — [Preprints.org 202112.0243](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202112.0243); [Computer Optics 47(3)](https://computeroptics.ru/eng/KO/Annot/KO47-3/470314e.html)
- Yan & Roy: multi-temporal weekly WELD input is a key component because it copes with crop rotations and cloud gaps — [OpenPrairie](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/gsce_pubs/45/)
- Waldner & Diakogiannis: a single S2 composite already gave high accuracy (DL), but "averaging model predictions from at least four images acquired across the season" was key to handling temporal variation — [arXiv 1910.12023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12023)
- Sentinel Hub NIVA: per-date predictions are merged per time interval with a per-pixel percentile (median by default), using only dates with under 5% cloud cover (verified in code) — [fd/post_processing.py](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation/blob/main/fd/post_processing.py)
- Tetteh et al. 2021 assessed how S1/S2 feature-set segmentation accuracy evolves as data accumulate from March to October. For S1, both polarisations plus two radar indices was the best set. Figure captions name S1BI, S2B10I and S2S1I as optimal on different test tiles, so the best set varies by tile — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9515974/); [Thünen PDF](https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn063902.pdf)
- García-Pedrero: NDVI, NDWI and SSI feature images for the superpixel merge classifier (secondary source) — [Medium](https://medium.com/geekculture/%D1%81r%D0%BE%D1%80-field-boundary-detection-approaches-and-main-challenges-46e37dd276bc)
- Single-date NDVI baseline: Gayibov used a single S2 NDVI layer and evaluated only qualitatively — [arXiv 2502.04529](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04529v1)
- A 2026 NAIP paper notes that crop development creates temporal contrast between adjacent fields even when a single date is ambiguous. It also notes that conventional edge methods confuse roads, tree lines, irrigation features and within-field texture with parcel boundaries — [arXiv 2607.21881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21881)

### Inferences
- A per-pixel max/min NDVI composite (the user's current input) collapses timing. Two adjacent fields with the same peak greenness but different crops or sowing dates (e.g. winter wheat vs. sunflower) can have nearly the same max NDVI, with only a small difference in min NDVI. Their boundary is visible in individual dates (e.g. May, when one field is green and the other is bare) but weak in max/min. Edges computed per date and then aggregated (sum, max or median of the gradient magnitude) keep these phenological-offset boundaries. This is the shared rationale of Watkins, Marszalek, SACFEI and Pavlova.
- Monthly (or 2–3-week) median composites from April to October, each with its own gradient, are a practical middle ground for cloud-prone Eastern European/Russian seasons. They are more robust than single dates and keep phenological contrast. The Sentinel Hub per-period median merging and Waldner's "at least 4 dates" are consistent with this.
- Useful additional channels, low cost and supported by cited methods: temporal std/CV of NDVI (North, SACFEI), NDWI or MSAVI2 alongside NDVI (Marszalek, Pavlova), and multi-year aggregated history to stabilise permanent boundaries such as roads and shelterbelts while ignoring within-season management patterns (Pavlova).
- Harmonic-regression coefficients and phenology metrics (SOS/EOS) make sense as region features for the merge criterion (similar temporal profile → same field), but no evidence was found that they beat simpler features as edge inputs.

### Gaps
- No ablation study was found that directly compares temporal-std NDVI, max/min/range composites, monthly composite stacks and harmonic coefficients for field-edge detection.
- The exact NDWI formulation in Marszalek et al. (McFeeters green–NIR or Gao NIR–SWIR) and the number of dates used were not confirmed.
- The final optimal S2 feature set in Tetteh et al. 2021 (which bands/indices, how many dates) was not confirmed from the full text.

## Q4. How accurate are classical methods compared with DL in comparative studies?

### Takeaway
With good input features and tuned parameters, classical/OBIA methods can come close to DL on medium/large European fields: optimised MRS reached F≈0.805, IoU≈0.678, against FracTAL ResUNet's 0.808 and 0.683 in Germany. They usually trail modern DL by a few to ~10 points elsewhere: OBIA about 82% OA vs R2U-Net 89% in NE China, and gPb-owt-ucm/MCG were beaten by FCN+OWT on smallholder fields. Their main weaknesses are small or elongated fields, weak boundaries between similar crops, and the need for regional parameter tuning.

### Cited Findings
- Tetteh et al. 2023 (PFG): optimised MRS scored an average F-score of 0.805 and IoU of 0.678. FracTAL ResUNet scored 0.808 and 0.683, against Mask R-CNN and U-Net in the same comparison. The authors conclude that DNNs, particularly FracTAL ResUNet, "can be effectively used for large-scale segmentation". Small or elongated fields reduce segmentation accuracy — [Springer PFG](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41064-023-00247-x); [EOLab summary](https://eolab.geo.hu-berlin.de/publication/tetteh_2023/)
- Zhang et al. 2021 (JAG 105, Heilongjiang, S2): R2U-Net OA about 89.28%, Kappa 0.85; residual U-Net about 85.78%; U-Net about 82.18%; traditional OBIA about 82%. Canny + watershed served as the automatic label generator — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4789dbeaf5e34c5bb9c589912e3ca44f). A secondary source quotes Canny + watershed at a mean OA of 93.76% in a related context — [ResearchGate R2U-Net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356267049_Automated_delineation_of_agricultural_field_boundaries_from_Sentinel-2_images_using_recurrent_residual_U-Net). This is unverified and conflicts with the ~82% OBIA figure, probably because of different metrics or sites.
- Persello et al. 2019: FCN + OWT + combinatorial grouping (F > 0.7 Nigeria, > 0.6 Mali, WorldView) outperformed the classical contour detectors gPb-owt-ucm, SCG and MCG — [PMC6737917](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6737917/)
- Watkins & van Niekerk 2019b: classical CEWS outperformed per-pixel supervised classification (MAE about 31 m lower, OA about 13.8% higher). This is a classical-vs-classical comparison — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169919310403)
- Graesser & Ramankutty 2017: cropland F-score 91%, low geometric error over 5,480 fields (no DL comparison) — [UBC record](https://open.library.ubc.ca/cIRcle/collections/52383/items/1.0378890)
- Pavlova et al. 2023: the multi-temporal approach "outperforms the state-of-the-art instant data approach" and needs much less training data than a neural-network method (abstract claims; no numbers seen) — [Computer Optics](https://computeroptics.ru/eng/KO/Annot/KO47-3/470314e.html)
- Wagner & Oppelt 2020b: a DL boundary detector + classical graph-based growing contours reached up to 88.2% accuracy and F1 0.88 — [Kiel repository](https://macau.uni-kiel.de/receive/macau_mods_00000670)
- Marszalek 2020 and Gayibov 2025 (GEE SNIC + Canny) report no quantitative boundary accuracy — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202004.0316/v1/download); [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2502.04529)
- Context on DL scale in the user's region: Delineate Anything Flow (YOLOv11-based instance segmentation) mapped Ukraine (603,000 km², S2 2024) in under 6 hours. It counted 3.75 M fields at 5 m and 5.15 M at 2.5 m, against 2.66 M by Sinergise and 1.69 M by NASA Harvest — [arXiv 2511.13417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13417v1)

### Inferences
- Most S2 comparisons on European fields put the classical-to-DL gap between near zero (well-tuned MRS) and about 7–10 OA points (generic OBIA). For large Russian/Kazakh steppe fields with strong shelterbelt/road boundaries, a well-tuned multi-temporal classical pipeline can plausibly come close to DL. The gap will be largest for small, elongated or similar-crop adjacent fields.
- Metrics differ between studies (OA, boundary F with tolerance, IoU, MAE in m, OSQ), so absolute numbers do not compare directly. A local benchmark with consistent metrics is needed to judge the user's pipeline.

### Gaps
- Exact per-method baseline numbers (gPb-owt-ucm, MCG) from Persello et al. 2019, and the Mask R-CNN/U-Net numbers from Tetteh 2023, were not accessible.
- No head-to-head classical vs DL comparison on Russian, Ukrainian or Kazakh Sentinel-2 data was found.
- The comprehensive review arXiv 2508.14558 (2025), which probably has a comparison table, could not be fetched — [arXiv 2508.14558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14558)

## Q6. Which published classical ideas could improve the user's pipeline (S2 seasonal max/min/range NDVI → SLIC → hierarchical agglomerative merge → ICM; oriented line filters for shelterbelts/roads; WorldCover cropland mask; Douglas–Peucker)?

### Takeaway
The highest-value changes supported by the literature are these. (1) Replace or augment the 3-band max/min/range composite with a multi-date edge stack (per-date or monthly gradient magnitude aggregated by median/sum) plus temporal-variability bands (local std/CV). (2) Make the merge criterion edge-driven and hierarchical (watershed hierarchy / UCM-style saliency via Higra, optionally with Structured Edges), or learned (García-Pedrero-style pairwise classifier). (3) Tune parameters by Bayesian optimisation against a small reference set (Tetteh). (4) Threshold line evidence by length (North) and refine polygons with active contours (Yan & Roy, Wagner & Oppelt) before simplification. Most of the current design (oriented line filters + over-segmentation + merge + relaxation + land-cover mask) has direct precedents in Rydberg & Borgefors, Graesser and North.

### Cited Findings
- Oriented filters + region over-segmentation + knowledge-based merging, which matches the user's oriented line filters + SLIC + merge — [Rydberg & Borgefors (Uppsala CBA)](https://www.cb.uu.se/annual_report/AR02html/node25.html)
- Thresholding edges by length rather than strength, with temporal local std + directional filters, gave district-scale operational use with "little need to vary parameters" — [North et al. abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329817494_Boundary_Delineation_of_Agricultural_Fields_in_Multitemporal_Satellite_Imagery)
- Multi-date edge aggregation: Watkins (aggregated multi-temporal edge layers, then watershed) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337182705_Automating_field_boundary_delineation_with_multi-temporal_Sentinel-2_imagery); Marszalek (Canny compiled across the season, NDWI) — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202004.0316/v1/download); SACFEI (per-date multi-kernel EGM, mean/median + CV) — [jgrss/sacfei](https://github.com/jgrss/sacfei)
- Probability relaxation of edge probabilities (relaxation labelling with compatibility coefficients over a neighbourhood) + CLAHE + multi-scale local adaptive thresholding + land-cover intersection (verified in code) — [jgrss/sacfei relax.py, utils.py](https://github.com/jgrss/sacfei)
- Learned merging: classifier trained on adjacent-segment pairs to drive agglomerative merging of over-segmented superpixels — [García-Pedrero (ULPGC)](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/handle/10553/55018); spatiotemporal consensus of segmentations — [Remote Sensing 10(12):1991](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10121991)
- Hierarchical merging with OWT weak-boundary enhancement gave parameters that were stable across regions — [Zhu et al. 2024 GF-AFD](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2023.2297947)
- Bayesian optimisation of segmentation parameters (supervised against reference parcels, or unsupervised with a modified metric); optimal values vary spatially and with field size — [Tetteh, Remote Sensing 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/18/3096/htm); [OpenAgrar PDF](https://www.openagrar.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/openagrar_derivate_00034378/dn062674.pdf)
- Active-contour refinement: Yan & Roy's variational region-based geometric active contour (no training data, few parameters) — [OpenPrairie](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/gsce_pubs/45/); Wagner & Oppelt's sub-pixel graph-based growing contours — [MDPI 12(7):1205](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/7/1205)
- UCM-style hierarchy tools available in Python: Higra watershed hierarchies + saliency map, demonstrated with the OpenCV Structured Edge gradient — [Higra-Notebooks](https://github.com/higra/Higra-Notebooks); [Higra](https://github.com/higra/Higra)
- Cropland masking alternatives: Otsu threshold on aggregated MSAVI2 as the "field detector" — [Pavlova et al. 2023](https://computeroptics.ru/eng/KO/Annot/KO47-3/470314e.html); crop-agriculture probability map as the first stage — [Yan & Roy 2016](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/gsce_pubs/38/)
- Temporal consensus of outputs: median of per-date results for each period — [Sentinel Hub fd/post_processing.py](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation/blob/main/fd/post_processing.py); average over at least 4 dates — [Waldner & Diakogiannis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12023)
- Weak-supervision path: classical Canny + watershed outputs used as automatic training labels for a DL model, which then exceeded OBIA (89% vs 82% OA) — [Zhang et al. 2021, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4789dbeaf5e34c5bb9c589912e3ca44f)
- Vectorisation with seamless tile merging and simplification (gdal_contour at 0.6, simplify 2.5) — [Sentinel Hub fd/vectorisation.py](https://github.com/sentinel-hub/field-delineation/blob/main/fd/vectorisation.py)

### Inferences
Concrete changes, in rough order of expected value and effort. These are recommendations derived from the findings above, not tested results.
1. **Multi-date edge stack instead of only max/min/range.** Build monthly (or 15-day) cloud-masked median NDVI and NDWI/MSAVI2 composites for April–October. Compute a gradient magnitude for each (Sobel/Scharr, or SACFEI's 5-kernel mean after Gaussian smoothing). Aggregate per pixel with median and max, and add the temporal CV of the gradient. Feed this aggregated edge map to the segmentation and merge steps. Keep max/min/range NDVI only as region features.
2. **Temporal-variability bands.** Add a local (3×3/5×5) standard deviation of NDVI averaged across dates, plus the per-pixel temporal std/CV of NDVI (North; SACFEI "VI CV"). Use them as an "inside-field homogeneity" term in the merge cost.
3. **Edge-aware over-segmentation.** Use watershed seeded on the aggregated edge map (CEWS-style), or SLIC/SNIC with low compactness on the edge-augmented stack, so superpixel borders snap to multi-date edges instead of the compactness grid.
4. **Hierarchical merge as a UCM.** Build a region adjacency graph weighted by mean aggregated edge strength along each shared border, combined with the temporal-profile distance between regions. Compute a watershed or agglomerative hierarchy (Higra), take the saliency (UCM) map, and choose the cut by Bayesian optimisation (e.g. Optuna) against about 100–300 manually digitised local fields. This is the OWT-UCM idea without a CNN. Optionally add OpenCV Structured Edges (a pretrained random forest, a "light classifier") on a 3-band false-colour composite as one more edge channel.
5. **Learned merge criterion (light classifier).** Following García-Pedrero, label a few hundred adjacent-superpixel pairs (same/different field) and train a Random Forest or GBM. Pair features: shared-border edge strength percentiles, differences in monthly NDVI profiles and harmonic/phenology metrics, area ratio, and a shelterbelt/road line-filter response on the border. Use its probability as the merge cost, with the existing ICM relaxation on top. This is "weak supervision" in the user's sense.
6. **Length-based acceptance of line evidence.** Keep the oriented line filters, but accept a shelterbelt or road edge mainly by connected length and straightness rather than contrast (North). Use these as hard "cannot-merge" constraints in the hierarchy.
7. **Better cropland mask.** Combine WorldCover with an Otsu threshold on multi-year aggregated MSAVI2/NDVI (Pavlova) or a season-amplitude criterion. WorldCover is a single 10 m land-cover product and can misclassify fallow or pasture.
8. **Multi-year stabilisation.** Running the segmentation on 2–3 seasons and keeping boundaries that persist (spatiotemporal consensus; Pavlova's historical aggregation) suppresses within-field management artefacts and recovers boundaries that are weak in a given year.
9. **Geometric refinement.** Before Douglas–Peucker, optionally refine each polygon with a region-based active contour (Chan–Vese, e.g. `skimage.segmentation.morphological_chan_vese`, in the spirit of Yan & Roy and Wagner & Oppelt) on the NDVI time-series stack. Then simplify with a tolerance of about 0.25–0.5 pixel (Sentinel Hub uses simplify 2.5 on a 2.5 m upsampled grid).
10. **Optional bridge to DL.** Use the improved classical polygons as automatic labels to train a small U-Net (the Zhang et al. 2021 pattern) if more accuracy is needed later.

### Gaps
- None of the cited studies measured the specific gain of replacing a max/min/range NDVI composite with a multi-date edge stack in a SLIC + agglomerative pipeline. The size of this improvement is not quantified and needs local testing.
- No published evaluation of Higra/UCM-style hierarchies specifically on Sentinel-2 field boundaries was found. The pattern is analogous to Persello's OWT + combinatorial grouping and to GF-AFD's hierarchical merging.
- No published optimal parameters (SLIC size/compactness, merge thresholds) were found for Russian or Kazakh steppe field sizes. Tetteh's results suggest they will vary spatially.
